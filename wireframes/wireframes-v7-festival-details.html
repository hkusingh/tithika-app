Ram Navami
Birth of Lord Rama
Chaitra · Shukla 9
Mon, Apr 27 2026
About
Ram Navami celebrates the birth of Lord Rama, the seventh avatar of Vishnu and the hero of the Ramayana. It falls on the ninth day of the bright fortnight of Chaitra, believed to be the moment Rama was born in Ayodhya to King Dasharatha and Queen Kaushalya.

The day marks the ideals of dharma, righteousness, and devotion that Lord Rama embodied throughout his life.
How to Observe
Fast from sunrise to sunset, breaking only after evening aarti
Visit Ram temples and attend kirtan or Ramayana recitation
Offer tulsi leaves, flowers, and panchamrit to Ram's idol
Read Sundarkand or Ramcharitmanas throughout the day
Participate in Rath Yatra processions in your community
Header gradient
8 preset gradients keyed by festival category (fire, saffron/devotion, water, earth, night, lotus, harvest, moon). Assigned in FestivalDetailSheet based on the canonical key. Zero added assets — pure Flutter painting.
Close button
Semi-transparent pill, top-right. Returns to festivals list. No back gesture conflict needed — modal sits above nav.
Date chips
Hindu date (paksha · tithi) + Gregorian date for current year, same logic as existing _FestivalRow.
Scrollable body
Header is fixed at 200dp. Body scrolls independently. On short phones, header compresses slightly via SliverAppBar collapse.
Hindi Devanagari example
Same sheet, same layout. Title becomes "राम नवमी", About and observances natively authored in Hindi — not translated. scriptStyle() dispatcher already handles font switching.

Hindu New Year
Chaitra Shukla 1 · Sat, Apr 18 2026
The Hindu New Year falls on the first day of Chaitra Shukla paksha. It is celebrated across India under three regional names — each with its own traditions.
Maharashtra
Gudi Padwa
Gudi Padwa marks the Marathi new year. A gudi — a bamboo staff topped with a bright cloth, neem leaves, and an inverted pot — is erected outside homes facing the rising sun as a symbol of victory and prosperity.
How to observe
Erect the Gudi at sunrise facing east
Eat neem leaves mixed with jaggery and tamarind for health
Prepare Shrikhand and Puran Poli as festival foods
Karnataka · Andhra Pradesh · Telangana
Ugadi
Ugadi ushers in the Telugu and Kannada new year. Homes are decorated with mango leaf toranams and the special Ugadi Pachadi — a dish combining six tastes — is prepared to symbolise the varied experiences of the coming year.
How to observe
Prepare and share Ugadi Pachadi with family
Decorate entrance with mango leaf toranam
North India
Chaitra Navratri
The nine-night festival of the goddess begins on this day. Devotees fast, observe daily aarti, and worship the nine forms of Durga (Navadurga) across nine consecutive days ending with Ram Navami.
How to observe
Keep a nine-day fast or phalahar (fruit) diet
Light an akhand diya and recite Durga Saptashati
Combined key detection
When the canonical festival key contains /, the sheet renders a sectioned layout instead of the single-festival layout. Each segment maps to its own JSON key: "Gudi Padwa", "Ugadi", "Chaitra Navratri".
Section layout
Each constituent festival gets a card with:
  • Region label (small, muted)
  • Festival title
  • 2–3 sentence description
  • Compact observances list
Future compatibility
When regional calendar lands, FestivalDetector returns "Gudi Padwa" or "Ugadi" directly based on the user's region. The combined layout becomes unused — each region naturally shows its own single-festival sheet.
Combined festivals in scope
· Gudi Padwa / Ugadi / Chaitra Navratri
· Baisakhi / Vishu
· Makar Sankranti / Pongal

Intro paragraph
One new key in AppStrings: ekadashiIntro(language). 2–3 sentences, natively written in each language. Sits above the month chip grid, inside the scroll area so it scrolls away.
No detail page for generic Ekadashi
Ekadashi date chips are not tappable — the intro paragraph provides all the context needed. Named Ekadashis (Nirjala, Devshayani, etc.) in the Festivals tab do open a detail sheet since they have their own history and specific observances.
Named Ekadashis in Festivals tab
Nirjala Ekadashi, Devshayani, Devutthana, Gita Jayanti — these appear in the Festivals tab and are fully tappable with their own detail page explaining their specific significance.
